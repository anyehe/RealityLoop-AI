# RealityLoop AI

An observable, adaptive AI survival agent.

## The Unique Kernel
RealityLoop AI makes an agent's failures and improvements observable. It connects decisions to outcomes, diagnoses problems, proposes changes, and tests whether those changes help.

## Who It's For
Developers, learners, and observers interested in evaluating AI behavior and understanding how an agent responds to mistakes rather than simply trusting its claims.

## The Core Loop
The observer starts or reviews a survival simulation. The agent observes resources and environmental conditions, selects an action, and receives an outcome from the simulation. It analyzes failures and risks, recommends and applies an improved strategy within defined limits, and provides evidence so the observer can compare results.

## Inspiration & Identity
An experimental survival environment with a transparent AI monitoring dashboard. The tone should be analytical, exploratory, and evidence-driven rather than sensational. Specific visual references are not established yet.

## Why This Matters to the Learner
Explore how AI agents adapt, improve strategies, and exhibit complex behavior—and how to distinguish measurable improvements from unsupported claims about intelligence or consciousness.

## What "Working" Looks Like
A user runs a 30-day survival scenario, inspects the agent's decisions and mistakes, and sees it change strategy after a problem occurs. Baseline and adaptive runs are compared using survival and resource-management metrics. The key demonstration is an agent diagnosing a mistake from recorded evidence, changing its priorities, and either performing better in a comparable test or explaining why its attempted improvement failed.

## The POC Boundary
- One simulated settlement and a 30-day survival challenge.
- Food, water, shelter, health, and a small set of unexpected events.
- An agent that observes state, selects actions, and evaluates outcomes.
- Bounded memory or records of previous outcomes.
- Feedback-driven strategy adaptation.
- Visible observations, decisions, diagnoses, and recommendations.
- Baseline-versus-adaptive comparison using measurable metrics.
- Basic automated tests and setup documentation.

## Later
- More complex environments and longer simulations.
- Additional agents and competing survival strategies.
- More advanced learning methods.
- Controlled experiments with strategy modification.
- Research into complex behavior and machine consciousness.

## Explicitly Cut
- Unrestricted self-modifying code: risky and unnecessary for proving feedback-driven adaptation.
- Claims of AI consciousness: behavioral adaptation alone cannot establish subjective experience.
- A full commercial survival game: beyond the first proof of concept.
- Multiplayer, combat, and large open worlds: not needed to demonstrate the core idea.
- Unverified claims of learning: changes and outcomes must be demonstrated with evidence.
